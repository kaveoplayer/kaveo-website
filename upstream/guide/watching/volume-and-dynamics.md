## What it is for

A song, an episode and a film can sound equally loud, a film can be watched quietly at night, and
voices can stand out from the music.

![The Volume and dynamics group in Settings, with Volume control on Software, Same loudness for every title on and its levelling rows, Night mode (compression) set to Medium and Dialogue boost set to +3 dB](../images/watching/volume-and-dynamics.webp)

## How to use it

1. In **Settings › Audio**, turn on **Same loudness for every title**; your local files are measured in the background while nothing plays. The rest is in [Same loudness for every title](same-loudness.md).
2. Set **Night mode (compression)** to **Light**, **Medium** or **Strong** to bring dialogue and explosions closer.
3. Set **Dialogue boost** to **+3 dB** or **+6 dB** to raise the voices.
4. While watching, right-click the picture and open **Audio** › **Sound** to change them at once.

## Good to know

- None acts on Dolby or DTS sent undecoded to a receiver with **Bitstream passthrough**.
- **Dialogue boost** needs 5.1 or 7.1 sound: stereo has no separate voice channel.
- The slider goes to 200 %; its middle, the original level, is the only bit-perfect one. Keys
  and the wheel stop there: press again to go higher. Past the middle that part turns
  from orange to red. Titles open at your last volume.
- With **Volume control** on **Output device**, the slider turns the device's own volume, so the
  sound stays bit-perfect below 100 %. It needs a chosen **Audio output device** with **Exclusive
  output** on. Its volume returns afterwards, even after a crash.
- Music levelled elsewhere (ReplayGain) plays at that level until Kaveo measures it, or for good
  with **Levelled from** on **ReplayGain tags**.
